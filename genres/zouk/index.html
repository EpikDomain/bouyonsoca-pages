Kassav’ and the Creole connection

Kassav’ is a key starting point for exploring zouk. Martinique's tourism authority connects the genre's international story with the group and identifies Jocelyne Béroard as an important voice in its spread. The music belongs within a wider Caribbean history, with Guadeloupe, Martinique and their diasporas central to that conversation.

Listening to a group and then to one of its members gives you two perspectives on that history. Our collection includes both Kassav’ and Jocelyne Béroard, with individual profiles that make the relationship clear.

A listening route across generations

Begin with the Kassav’ profile, then move to Jocelyne Béroard's recordings. Follow those with K-Reen and Fanny J to explore voices connected to zouk and R&B. Those connections do not make every recording identical in style; the profiles and their music links are there to help you hear the differences.

Try listening once for the voice, once for the rhythm section, and once for the arrangement around the chorus. This is an editorial listening exercise, not a definition of how every zouk song must sound. Let the individual recordings lead.

More than a single label

The first collection is intentionally small enough to explore in one sitting. It introduces four artists or groups rather than claiming to map the whole genre. Official artist information, music catalog entries and cultural sources are linked from each profile.

Keep going through the other genre guides. Bouyon opens a route into Dominica's bands and solo performers; soca connects you to four Trinidadian acts. Together, the guides let you travel across related scenes while keeping the names, places and histories visible.

A few good questions.

Where should a new zouk listener start?

Start with Kassav’, then explore Jocelyne Béroard's individual profile. K-Reen and Fanny J offer further routes through zouk and its connections with R&B.

Why include Jocelyne Béroard separately from Kassav’?

Her work as a member of Kassav’ and her individual recordings give listeners distinct ways to explore her contribution. The two profiles are connected rather than presented as unrelated acts.
