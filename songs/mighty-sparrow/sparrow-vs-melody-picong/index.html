Behind the recording

Sparrow vs. Melody Picong — the live contest in First Flight

“Sparrow vs. Melody Picong” is the live verbal contest between Mighty Sparrow and Lord Melody described in Gordon Rohlehr’s First Flight notes. Its writing credit names Slinger Francisco and Fitzroy Alexander.

What makes this a picong performance?

Rohlehr presents the exchange as a live contest between Sparrow and Lord Melody within an established calypso practice of sharp verbal insults. His account notes that the insults can be coarse and improvised.

There is an important qualification: he describes Sparrow’s lines as probably memorized. Calling the performance picong therefore does not establish that every line was invented on the spot. Its live setting, two named opponents and joint writing credit distinguish this recording from a solo studio song.

Sources: Smithsonian Folkways — First Flight: Early Calypsos from the Emory Cook Collection booklet

A joint writing credit and a live performance

The booklet prints 1957 beside this track and references Jump Up Carnival. It is the exception to the booklet’s general Slinger Francisco writing credit, adding Fitzroy Alexander for this selection.
