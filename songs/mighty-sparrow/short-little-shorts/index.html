Behind the recording

Short Little Shorts — Mighty Sparrow: commentary and edition dates

Gordon Rohlehr’s notes for “Short Little Shorts” describe a governor who abolishes or fails to apply an indecent-exposure law, provoking criticism over short clothing. The booklet assigns the song a 1958 header year.

Who is the target of the joke?

In Rohlehr’s reading, the governor changes or declines to enforce the indecent-exposure law after seeing a woman in Bermuda shorts. The joke includes the governor’s own interest in looking at her, which undermines the posture of moral authority.

Rohlehr extends that reading to moral policing and the censorship of sexuality. This is commentary on a comic song and its characters; the source does not establish a particular governor’s identity, location or an actual change in legislation.

Sources: Smithsonian Folkways — First Flight: Early Calypsos from the Emory Cook Collection booklet

Why two earlier years appear

The song header names King Sparrow’s Calypso Carnival. Elsewhere the same booklet lists that earlier album as a 1959 edition. A song year and an album-edition year describe different references; neither establishes an exact session date.
