Behind the recording

Post Card to Sparrow — Mighty Sparrow: the Christmas-card narrative

“Post Card to Sparrow” is described in Gordon Rohlehr’s commentary as a Christmas-card narrative about a lover who remained at home. The First Flight booklet prints 1958 beside the song.

How does the Christmas card frame the story?

Rohlehr describes a narrator away from a lover who has stayed at home. Reading her Christmas card gives the song its setting of separation and seasonal nostalgia. The commentary does not place the lover in America.

He contrasts this affectionate, sentimental mode with the cynical treatment of romance he finds in much of Sparrow’s early repertoire. That comparison explains the song’s place in the selection without treating a fictional narrator’s circumstances as a biographical account of the singer.

Sources: Smithsonian Folkways — First Flight: Early Calypsos from the Emory Cook Collection booklet

From an earlier album to First Flight

The track header references King Sparrow’s Calypso Carnival, listed elsewhere in the booklet as a 1959 edition. The two printed years are retained with their separate meanings; neither is presented as an exact recording date.
