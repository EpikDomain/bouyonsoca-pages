Behind the recording

No, Doctor, No — Mighty Sparrow: title, context and First Flight edition

Mighty Sparrow’s “No, Doctor, No” also appears under the title “The Situation in Trinidad.” Gordon Rohlehr’s First Flight commentary relates the song to public response to income taxation.

What is the taxation argument in the song?

Gordon Rohlehr reads the song as a reversal: its narrator begins with a threatening attitude toward the new politicians, then backs away from violence. In this interpretation, Sparrow turns public anger about taxation toward acceptance of paying taxes.

Rohlehr discusses it alongside “PAYE” and “You Can’t Get Away from the Tax.” That comparison concerns a sequence of political songs; it is not evidence that the events spoken by the narrator happened to Sparrow himself.

Sources: Smithsonian Folkways — First Flight: Early Calypsos from the Emory Cook Collection booklet

When did PAYE begin?

Trinidad and Tobago’s Inland Revenue Division dates the introduction of PAYE to 1958. This is the tax-withholding system’s start, not the first year of income taxation. The booklet’s 1957 song header and that 1958 policy date therefore identify different things.

Sources: Trinidad and Tobago Inland Revenue Division — PAYE history · Smithsonian Folkways — First Flight: Early Calypsos from the Emory Cook Collection booklet

The 1957 song reference

The booklet prints 1957 beside the song and points to Calypso Kings and Pink Gin. It credits the writing to Slinger Francisco. These references identify the song’s earlier context without supplying an exact recording day.
