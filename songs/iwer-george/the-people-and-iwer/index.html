The People and Iwer — the choir, four approaches and seven tracks

Iwer George wanted a choir to represent the people in “The People and Iwer.” His account explains how that collaboration began; the seven-track edition makes the named versions easier to choose.

  • Why does Iwer describe four versions while the release contains seven tracks?

Finding Voix Riches

Iwer told Newsday that he contacted Queen’s Hall when he wanted a choir. Its manager referred him to the southern group Voix Riches, whom he then brought into the studio. He described the collaboration as a way of returning the affection audiences had shown him.

He said writing had begun two years earlier and production took three months. These are retrospective intervals, not exact session dates.

Sources: Newsday — Iwer interview

Four approaches and seven indexed tracks

The interview describes chutney, jab jab, live and acoustic approaches. The captured edition uses “Power Soca” as one of its labels and adds extended tracks to three of the four groups. The interview’s broad version count and the seven-track listing therefore describe different levels of detail; no unlabelled Jab Jab track is invented.

Announcement and catalog date

The report published December 26, 2022 announces release that day; Apple dates the captured edition December 28. The sources establish an announcement and a provider edition date, without explaining the two-day difference. “Live” is a version label here; no concert venue or audience recording is established.
