Behind the recording

So Long — Nadia Batson’s reunion concept and the Purple Heart Riddim

Nadia Batson built “So Long” around recognizing someone she had not seen for a long time. Her account connects that conversational idea to an earlier writing request from Farmer Nappy and the rhythm they eventually shared.

  • How did Hookin’ Meh lead to So Long, and who is credited on Batson’s recording?

A commission became an invitation

In her January 2019 Newsday interview, Batson recalled Farmer Nappy asking her around mid-2018 to write on a Red Boyz rhythm. That request produced “Hookin Meh.” After hearing it, he encouraged her to create something for herself on the same rhythm.

Batson described imagining how a Trinidadian would greet an old friend, then turning that encounter into a song. She remembered completing the writing in one studio session and thought it took less than two hours. That duration is her estimate, rather than a timed session record.

Sources: Nadia Batson — So Long writing interview, Newsday

The provider lists several writing contributions

Batson’s account explains her concept; it is not a statement that she was the only contributor. Apple’s selected song page distinguishes lyric-writing, composition and production roles. “Lyrics” below is the provider’s authorship label, not a reproduction of the words sung.

Find both recordings on the shared release

The selected edition of The Purple Heart Riddim - Single places Farmer Nappy’s “Hookin’ Meh” first and Batson’s “So Long” second. Its October 19, 2018 edition date belongs to this package; it does not date the writing session.
