Behind the recording

Siwo — Jocelyne Béroard: her writing account and album/live versions

Jocelyne Béroard links “Siwo” to a sequencer idea from Jacob Desvarieux and to her own decision to write romance from a woman’s perspective. Her account helps explain the song; the catalog listings below help distinguish its album and live appearances.

How Béroard describes the song’s beginnings

In her 2019 interview with Pan African Music, Jocelyne Béroard recalls hearing Jacob Desvarieux experiment with a sequencer. She asked to develop the idea, and he reworked it into “Siwo.” This is her account of the song’s creation; the interview does not identify the machine or date the session.

Béroard explains that she wrote from a woman’s perspective, reversing a romantic convention usually expressed by men. She also connects the song to her rejection of pressure to prefer lighter-skinned partners and to her wish to value men from her own community. That explanation gives the song a specific personal and social context without turning its narrator into a factual biography.

Sources: Pan African Music — Jocelyne Béroard interview

Writing and recording credits

Apple Music’s Siwo track entry credits Desvarieux for composition and Béroard for composition and lyrics. Kassav’ is credited as recording engineer. These are the roles attached to this particular album entry; they do not establish the personnel of a later live performance.

Find the named edition or performance

For the album track, choose the Siwo listing. The Chawa entry explicitly identifies a live performance at the Folies Bergère, while the Kolé Séré single package also contains a track titled “Siwo.” Matching titles alone do not establish that the two non-live entries use the same master.

Béroard recalls releasing her album in December 1986. Apple’s captured Siwo edition instead displays January 1, 1986. Those two dates remain separate observations; neither is used here as a settled worldwide first-release date.
