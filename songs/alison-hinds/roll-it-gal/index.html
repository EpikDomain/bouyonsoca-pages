Behind the recording

Roll It Gal — Shontelle’s writing account and Alison Hinds’s named mixes

“Roll It Gal” can be approached through two firsthand accounts: Shontelle explains the writing opportunity, while Alison Hinds describes what the song means in her performances. The selected single also provides three clearly labeled listening choices.

  • How did Roll It Gal reach Alison Hinds, and which mix is the selected release offering?

The track supplied to Shontelle

In her Blues & Soul interview with Pete Lewis, Shontelle recalled meeting Hinds’s manager through the network around Sonya Mullins. He supplied tracks for her to write to while Hinds was preparing an album.

Shontelle said she selected a beat from the CD and finished her song in about fifteen minutes. A later studio call told her Hinds wanted to record it. The interview calls the song “Roll It”; this account concerns the song she wrote for Hinds, not proof that all recordings using that shorter title are the same master.

Sources: Shontelle — writing Roll It for Alison Hinds, Blues & Soul interview

How Hinds describes its role onstage

Asked by Black Enterprise which songs remained exciting to perform, Hinds identified “Roll It Gal” as an anthem for female empowerment. She described messages from women about its encouraging effect and said she did not tire of performing it.

That is the performer’s account of the song’s reception. It does not establish a measured audience effect or an objective popularity ranking.

Sources: Alison Hinds — Roll It Gal performance and empowerment interview, Black Enterprise

The selected single contains three labeled entries

Apple’s US Roll It Gal - Single edition is dated August 27, 2007. Its unsuffixed song page separately displays January 1, 2005, while the Rishi Rich Remix page displays July 24, 2006. The dates are retained as provider observations rather than resolved into a worldwide first-release date.
