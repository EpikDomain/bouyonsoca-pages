Behind the recording

Fantastic Friday — Superblue’s studio choices and performance idea

“Fantastic Friday” was shaped for performance. Superblue and his studio collaborator described both the ideas they developed and the backing vocals they decided to remove.

  • How did Superblue and Nelson develop Fantastic Friday before its 2013 Carnival run?

From guitar melody to studio arrangement

In Debbie Jacob’s Guardian interview, Superblue recalled bringing a guitar melody to Juelio Nelson at Nu Generations Studios in St James. They shaped the song during December, drawing on material he had developed since Trinidad and Tobago’s 2006 World Cup journey.

Their choices included an opening Viking horn and Catholic bells. They considered a choir in the bridge but ultimately removed background vocals that they felt did not fit. These comments describe the development process; they do not identify a final choir or prove the source of any sample.

Sources: Guardian — Superblue interview

Water, powder and a song built for the stage

Superblue connected the water-and-powder idea to sailor masquerade and street theatre. His manager Gregory Fernandez separately described a performer who structured the song with its stage presentation in mind.

Fernandez’s February 2013 account reports a January 1 launch. The studio collaborator appears as Juelio Nelson in the Guardian and Julian Nelson, also called Julio, in Newsday; those source spellings are preserved here.

Sources: Guardian — Superblue interview · Newsday — Fernandez interview

Choose the compilation entry deliberately

Apple’s Soca Gold 2013 entry is titled “Fantastic Friday (De Bouncin’ Start)” and carries May 17, 2013. That is a selected compilation entry’s date, after the reported January launch. Its title alone does not establish whether this edition is identical to every earlier release.
