  • Cousin Family — Lord Invader: the Felix ensemble recording

    Lord Invader’s “Cousin Family” appears with Felix and his Internationals on Calypso in New York. The booklet distinguishes it from a different Destroyer song bearing the same title.

  • God Made Us All — Lord Invader and Pete Seeger: the 1946 performance

    “God Made Us All” brings Lord Invader and Pete Seeger together in the Smithsonian’s Calypso in New York selection. The session header identifies the Union Hootenanny in New York on May 9, 1946.

  • I’m Going Back to Africa — Lord Invader: the 1961 New York version

    The performance of “I’m Going Back to Africa” closing Calypso in New York was recorded in New York on June 6, 1961. The booklet contrasts it with a Paris recording from 1956.

  • Labor Day (Jump in the Line) — Lord Invader: the circa 1948 recording

    “Labor Day” appears as “Labor Day (Jump in the Line)” in the Calypso in New York booklet. The documented New York recording dates to approximately April 1948.

  • My Experience on the Reperbahn — Lord Invader: the second version

    The booklet calls this “My Experience on the Reperbahn (2nd version).” It is the Lord Invader and his Calypso Group performance selected as track 20 of Calypso in New York.

  • My Intention Is War — Lord Invader and Mighty Dictator: recording credits

    “My Intention Is War” is credited to Lord Invader and Mighty Dictator in the Smithsonian tracklist. Its session notes place both singers in a New York recording from around 1958.

  • New York Subway — Lord Invader: session musicians and earlier issues

    “New York Subway” is documented as a Lord Invader performance with Felix and his Internationals, recorded in New York around January 1946. The session notes name both its clarinet and cuatro players.

  • The Soldiers Came and Broke Up My Life — Lord Invader: version 2

    The Calypso in New York booklet labels this performance “The Soldiers Came and Broke Up My Life (#2).” The current label tracklist shortens the title to “The Soldiers Came and Broke My Life.”

  • Tied-Tongue Baby — Lord Invader: credits and title connections

    Lord Invader’s “Tied-Tongue Baby” is track 12 of Calypso in New York. Its booklet places the Felix and his Internationals recording around January 1946 in New York.

  • You’re Wasting Your Time — Lord Invader: the 1961 alternative take

    The Smithsonian booklet identifies this selection as “You’re Wasting Your Time (#2) (alternative take).” It dates the New York recording to June 6, 1961.

  • No, Doctor, No — Mighty Sparrow: title, context and First Flight edition

    Mighty Sparrow’s “No, Doctor, No” also appears under the title “The Situation in Trinidad.” Gordon Rohlehr’s First Flight commentary relates the song to public response to income taxation.

  • No More Rocking — Mighty Sparrow: calypso context and First Flight credits

    Gordon Rohlehr places “No More Rocking” against calypso’s international popularity in 1957, including Harry Belafonte’s recordings. The First Flight track header gives Sparrow’s song a 1958 year.

  • Post Card to Sparrow — Mighty Sparrow: the Christmas-card narrative

    “Post Card to Sparrow” is described in Gordon Rohlehr’s commentary as a Christmas-card narrative about a lover who remained at home. The First Flight booklet prints 1958 beside the song.

  • Russian Satellite — Mighty Sparrow: Sputnik context and edition history

    Gordon Rohlehr’s First Flight commentary connects “Russian Satellite” to a dog carried aboard Sputnik. The track header prints 1958 and names King Sparrow’s Calypso Carnival as its earlier album reference.

  • Short Little Shorts — Mighty Sparrow: commentary and edition dates

    Gordon Rohlehr’s notes for “Short Little Shorts” describe a governor who abolishes or fails to apply an indecent-exposure law, provoking criticism over short clothing. The booklet assigns the song a 1958 header year.

  • Sparrow vs. Melody Picong — the live contest in First Flight

    “Sparrow vs. Melody Picong” is the live verbal contest between Mighty Sparrow and Lord Melody described in Gordon Rohlehr’s First Flight notes. Its writing credit names Slinger Francisco and Fitzroy Alexander.
