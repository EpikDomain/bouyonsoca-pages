Behind the music

The story so far.

Mighty Sparrow was the stage name of Slinger Francisco, born in Grenada in 1935 and raised in Trinidad and Tobago. The University of the West Indies traces his 1956 Calypso King and Road March victories to Jean and Dinah, and records its own award of an honorary Doctor of Letters in 1987. His work used calypso to address politics and everyday Caribbean life. The artist’s official website states that he died on September 27, 2026. For an introduction to the archive, First Flight: Early Calypsos from the Emory Cook Collection brings together 18 tracks in the documented 2005 edition. Its opening entries include No, Doctor, No (The Situation in Trinidad) and Sparrow Vs. Melody Picong. That compilation date identifies the edition, not the year every performance was recorded. Credits, versions and context

BEHIND THE SONGS.

  • No, Doctor, No — Mighty Sparrow: title, context and First Flight edition

    Mighty Sparrow’s “No, Doctor, No” also appears under the title “The Situation in Trinidad.” Gordon Rohlehr’s First Flight commentary relates the song to public response to income taxation.

  • No More Rocking — Mighty Sparrow: calypso context and First Flight credits

    Gordon Rohlehr places “No More Rocking” against calypso’s international popularity in 1957, including Harry Belafonte’s recordings. The First Flight track header gives Sparrow’s song a 1958 year.

  • Post Card to Sparrow — Mighty Sparrow: the Christmas-card narrative

    “Post Card to Sparrow” is described in Gordon Rohlehr’s commentary as a Christmas-card narrative about a lover who remained at home. The First Flight booklet prints 1958 beside the song.

  • Russian Satellite — Mighty Sparrow: Sputnik context and edition history

    Gordon Rohlehr’s First Flight commentary connects “Russian Satellite” to a dog carried aboard Sputnik. The track header prints 1958 and names King Sparrow’s Calypso Carnival as its earlier album reference.

  • Short Little Shorts — Mighty Sparrow: commentary and edition dates

    Gordon Rohlehr’s notes for “Short Little Shorts” describe a governor who abolishes or fails to apply an indecent-exposure law, provoking criticism over short clothing. The booklet assigns the song a 1958 header year.

  • Sparrow vs. Melody Picong — the live contest in First Flight

    “Sparrow vs. Melody Picong” is the live verbal contest between Mighty Sparrow and Lord Melody described in Gordon Rohlehr’s First Flight notes. Its writing credit names Slinger Francisco and Fitzroy Alexander.
